Six patterns that keep showing up

  1. Borrow a gesture people know

    Muji's pull cord, the FlyEase heel kick, Nest's turning ring. When the motion is familiar, nobody needs a manual.

  2. Design for the edges

    OXO started with arthritic hands. The Xbox controller started with gamers who play with their feet. What helps them helps everyone.

  3. Move the heavy part

    Dyson put the motor in the handle. Fellow counterweighted the kettle. Where the weight sits decides how a thing feels.

  4. Make the limit the feature

    Playdate's black-and-white screen, the tripod's bottle-sized fold, Balmuda's 5 cc of water. Limits make products memorable.

  5. Give it a home

    The Pencil charges where it rests. The Always Pan holds its own spatula. Good design includes where a thing lives.

  6. Look outside your field

    A woodworking rasp became the Microplane. A sawmill gave Dyson his cyclone. A burr on a dog gave us Velcro. Good ideas travel.

One more thing

Almost none of these products started as a big idea. They started with a small annoyance that someone refused to ignore: a peeler that hurt, a thermostat nobody understood, a bag that let the rain in.

You're good at spotting those annoyances. Write them down. Keep sketching. One day something you made will belong in a collection like this, and I'll get to say I knew you first.

Happy birthday, AAyush. Here's to a year full of good problems.

— Jayaraj
