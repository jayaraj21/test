One clever move per object

Six patterns hiding in this drawer

  1. Borrow a gesture people already know

    Muji's pull cord, the FlyEase heel kick, Nest's dial. Reuse a motion from somewhere else and nobody needs a manual.

  2. Design for the edge, win the middle

    OXO started with arthritic hands. The Xbox controller started with gamers who play with their feet. Everyone benefits.

  3. Move the heavy part

    Dyson put the motor in the handle. Fellow counterweighted the kettle. Where the mass sits decides how a thing feels.

  4. Make the constraint the feature

    Playdate's 1-bit screen, the Travel Tripod's water-bottle width, Balmuda's 5 cc of water. Limits make products memorable.

  5. Let the object store itself

    The Pencil charges where it parks. The Always Pan holds its own spatula. Good design includes where the thing lives.

  6. Show the inside

    Freitag's scuffed tarps, Fairphone's screws, Nothing's lit-up back. Honesty about how a thing is made can be the whole brand.

One more thing

None of these products started as a big idea. They started as a small annoyance someone refused to ignore: a peeler that hurt, a thermostat that was ugly, a bag that got wet in the rain.

You're good at noticing those small annoyances. Keep a list. Keep sketching. One day a product of yours belongs in a drawer like this, and I get to say I knew you before.

Have a brilliant birthday, and a year full of good problems to solve.

— Jayaraj
